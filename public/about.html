How it happened

Two home bakeries walked into one storefront

Plainfield natives Kim Whisenhunt and Michaela Gallaher met through their craft: Kim baking bagels as Blue Heron Bagels (launched 2024), Michaela baking sourdough as Gallaher Sourdough (running since 2023). Both had the problem every successful home baker eventually gets — demand outrunning a kitchen.

Michaela ran her cottage bakery full-time; sellouts sometimes came within thirty minutes of orders opening. Kim, a teacher of the deaf and hard-of-hearing until this leap, kept hitting the walls of a home kitchen — bagels with cream cheese are a commercial-kitchen thing. The demand got “unbearable” in the nicest possible way, and a shared commercial kitchen started to sound like the answer.

The pair began dreaming up the business in August 2026. When the Lockport Street storefront — a historic downtown space whose recent tenants included Milette’s Cakes, in a building with a long history of bakeries — came available, they signed the lease on September 15.

“To find a historic building where there is a history of bakeries here, and it’s in the heart of downtown, all of the pieces fit together so well… Plainfield history is part of both of our stories, and we’ll be incorporating that into the vibe and decor of the Bakehouse.”

Michaela Gallaher & Kim Whisenhunt, to Patch and What Now (2026)

How the shop works

It’s a hybrid by design: the owners share the space and the kitchen, but each baker operates their own side with separate baking staff — Kim the bagelry, Michaela the breads, sweets and pastries — while partnering on the front of the shop and sharing profits from coffee, books and merchandise. The bookshop in the front window is Michaela’s brainchild, filling a gap downtown has been missing.

What the shop stands for

No tips. Fair wages first: the team shouldn’t depend on inconsistent tips, so customers who want to tip are pointed at the Plainfield Food Pantry or an honest Google review instead. No gluten-free, on purpose — it isn’t legal to produce gluten-free goods in a gluten-filled kitchen, and they won’t bake something hazardous for the people who need it; ask at the counter for a referral. And no drama: two friends, one shared kitchen, one historic bakery block, a morning blend crafted with neighboring Krema Coffee House because downtown is better as a team.

Where it stands now

Permitting cleared in October 2026; local contractors have been rebuilding the space, documented on social as it happens. Final inspections are targeted for the first week of December 2026, then a soft opening, then the official grand opening after New Year’s. Planned hours at launch: Tuesday through Sunday, 7 a.m. to 1 p.m., with room for roughly ten to twelve guests to sit.
